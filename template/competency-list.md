# What I can do

Write this first, before you study anything. Five to fifteen lines, each starting **"I can…"** and describing something you *do*, not something you know about.

> ❌ "Learn X"
> ✅ "I can do Y, from a blank page, without looking anything up, in N minutes."

**Phrasing by track** (from the intake): A — the *exact* performance you will be asked for, in its real format and time. B — small enough to succeed today; read your first list to your mentor before you start. C — your best guess, marked `?`, revised at two weeks.

**A line is done when the proof exists, not when you feel ready.** Feeling ready is what familiarity feels like. Pick the proof off this menu; if you can't pick one, the line is still a topic — make it smaller.

| Proof | What you actually do |
|---|---|
| Build it again blind | close everything, produce it from an empty page |
| Say it out loud | two minutes, no notes, how it works and why you chose it |
| Show the mistake | what broke, what you thought was happening, what was |
| Get it reviewed | send it to the answer-key source; write down what changed |
| Prove it stays fixed | a test or check that fails before and passes after |

**Score** (Track A, and anyone with a deadline): mark each line at baseline and again before the deadline, dated. The gap is the plan.

**When you touch this file:** end of every session — tick what you proved, add `?` lines for anything that came up. Weekly review — collect the `?` lines, the undone lines, the ones you now think were a bad idea. Decide then, not mid-week.

This list is yours. In a mentored path the mentor reads it and never writes in it.
